Who I am

Writer, Scholar,
Arts Programmer

Soumya Rachel Shailendra is a PhD candidate in Comparative Literary Studies with a home department in Asian Languages and Cultures, specializing in postcolonial theory, modern South Asian literature, and the histories of caste radicalism.

Her dissertation examines how law and literature serve as important sites of reform and redress in the global anti-caste movement. Working within a multilingual and multimodal archive, she investigates how Dalit writers — authors from historically marginalized castes — deploy legal idioms to challenge their incorporation into institutions of liberal statehood.

She holds a B.A. in Literature and Drama from Bennington College, Vermont, where she was a founding student member of the Mellon Consortium on Forced Migration, Displacement, and Education (CFMDE). She also studied at Middlebury College, and has previously interned with the MANOA journal at the University of Hawai'i Press.

Soumya co-directs Translators Adda, a public-facing translation workshop series focused on critical issues in South Asian politics and culture across the Chicagoland region. Her collaborative work with the SpaceShift Collective raises awareness around caste violence and anti-caste cultural practice in Chicago.

Research

Dissertation Project

Law, Literature & the Global Anti-Caste Movement

This dissertation examines how law and literature function as intertwined sites of reform and redress in the global anti-caste movement. Working within a multilingual, multimodal archive, the project investigates how Dalit writers deploy legal idioms — rights claims, constitutional language, juridical procedure — to contest their incorporation into institutions of liberal statehood. Texts span Hindi, Marathi, English, and Malayalam, engaging a transnational archive of anti-caste thought.

I

Black Radicalism & Anti-Caste Solidarity

Tracing the transracial solidarity between the Black Power and anti-caste movements of the early 1970s, examining the "inner life" of caste in Dalit and African American writings.

II

Mourning, Lamentation & Caste Memory

Interrogating the affective structures of caste through the sonic and formal registers of lamentation and mourning rituals in twentieth-century African American and Dalit literatures.

III

Dalit Epistemologies & Liberation Theologies

Exploring how oppressed communities construct alternative knowledge systems and spiritual-political frameworks outside dominant institutions, drawing on decolonial thought.
